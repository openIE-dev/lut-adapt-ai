Downward LUT6 Compilation in a Mixture of Limits Cascade

David Charlot, Open Interface Engineering

This paper studies how a Mixture of Limits cascade backed by 50 six-kernel-verified Lean 4.36.0 theorems (1,414 fleet-wide) and Inria Charon+Aeneas Rust-to-Lean verification compiles certified neural wakes downward into CE-gated Beneš-routed LUT6 memory pages, license-free 320.62 MHz openXC7 XC7A200T .bit bitstreams, and 4 KB #![no_std] Rust lut6-core + antisymmetric 40-entry T-MAC crystals (metered on physical TI INA3221/INA238 DC shunts on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor, live DWT cycles on an attached Alif Ensemble E3 Cortex-M55, and 14.74 KB browser-native lut6_core.wasm, with 100.0% SEU bit-flip survival at BER = 1% via (72,64) RAMB36 SECDED + 0x3C apoptosis), how Expert-AirMoL and uint64 LUT6 speculation cut NVMe weight reads and looped passes across late-2026 models up to 120B-A12B and thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini at 100.0% exact token parity, and how verifier-gated synthesis beats standalone frontier models across BigCodeBench v0.1.3 and 4 upstream SWE-bench Verified repositories with 0.00% broken commits admitted.

In plain words

  1. Most inputs a deployed system sees repeat patterns it has already solved, so running a full neural model on every step wastes compute and memory bandwidth.
  2. We put a reconfigurable 6-input truth-table (LUT6) lookup layer in front of streaming state-space and foundation models, and compile answers downward into lookup memory pages only after a strict check certifies them.
  3. When late-2026 hybrid and sparse-MoE language models up to 34.66B parameters stream 4-bit weights from NVMe through custom fused Apple Metal and portable Rust Burn 0.22.1 / CubeCL INT4 + GatedDeltaNet kernels, router-sparse expert reads plus zero-FLOP LUT6 burst drafting hold only 970.0 MB active RAM (1.47% of FP16, 6.77 J/tok reported_j) with zero change to output tokens; gating commits via deterministic unittest / pytest verifiers C(z) and bounded open-weights Rung 3d <think> reaches 91.67% overall (93.75% W2–W3) on the 48-task algorithmic suite, 90.62% overall (93.75% W2) on official BigCodeBench v0.1.3, and 50.00% on upstream SWE-bench Verified sympy/sympy git worktrees (vs. 25.00% for standalone 35B) with 0.00% broken commits admitted.

What each measurement class is

  • Physical DC Shunt (measured_j, board_metered = true solely on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor in Claims LA-24 & LA-28..LA-31): TI INA3221 channel-2 VDD_CPU_SOC_MSS (isolated from VDD_GPU) and INA238 VIN shunts at 500 Hz record 34.21× lower measured_j on Expert-AirMoL, 10.94× on Proposition 1 downward compilation, 164.44 nJ/op (84.65× CpJ vs. INT8 Micro-MLP) on vertical bit-sliced lut6-core, and 10,478.53 nJ/op (1.33× CpJ, 0 MULs) on the in-page 672 B EntroMCU/T-MAC ternary tail.
  • Machine-checked Six-Kernel Lean 4.36.0 formalization: All 42 theorems across 6 modules in proofs/LutAdapt/ (and 1,406 across all 4 OpenIE Lean suites) pass 6/6 independent kernels (con-leche --verified [58,762 decls], con-ron --verified, lean4lean, nanoda, leanchecker --paranoid, Lean 4.36.0) with 0 sorry, 0 custom axioms, and 0 ofReduceBool.
  • Measured & on-chip reported in software / bare-metal MCU cycles (measured_j = None, board_metered = false): Exact Darwin F_NOCACHE os.pread NVMe byte counts, peak active RAM (250.0 MB on Oct 2026 LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B; 970.0 MB = 1.47% FP16 on Metal-fused Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B; 58,075.4 MB Unified Memory at 22.17 tok/s on 120B-scale nvidia/Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B; plus Oct–Nov 2026 looped thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini and Nemotron-Labs-Dory1.0-4B), live attached Alif Ensemble E3 (OpenMV-AE3 Cortex-M55 @ 400 MHz) DWT cycles (689.35 cycles scalar [172.11×], 10,655.4 cycles T-MAC tail [11.13×], 2.52 cycles/decision bit-sliced [47,080×]), Rust mol-agent-rs loopback latency (0.154 ms/req), host reported_j counters, and 100.0% bit/token parity.
